Unpaid toll in France: fines and scam texts

Missing a gantry happens to everyone, and the law allows for it: as long as you act quickly, the extra cost stays at €10. Here is the exact timeline, how to dispute a notice, and how to spot the scam texts that play on this fear.

Legal texts checked on 21 October 2026.

Only one notice per journey, even after several gantries: a journey means the same operator, the same direction, over 24 hours at most. Beyond five offences in twelve months, non-payment becomes a criminal offence punishable by a €7,500 fine.

Legal texts: French Code of Criminal Procedure, articles 529-6, R49-8-4-1 and R49-7; French Highway Code, articles R419-2 and L419-1.

How to dispute a payment notice

The notice includes a dispute card, to be returned within two months to the address shown, with your supporting documents. Three cases are provided for:

  • The vehicle was lent or hired: include the name, address and driving licence number of the person driving, and the hire agreement if there is one.
  • Theft, sale or cloned plates: include the police report receipt or the declaration of sale.
  • Any other reason, for example a toll already paid: include your written explanation and proof of payment.

Keep the receipt of every online payment: it is your best proof. The dispute card is laid down by article A37-33 of the French Code of Criminal Procedure.

Scam toll texts: how to spot them

Text messages claim alleged unpaid tolls, with a link to a copy of an official website. They play on the fear of extra charges.

  • A genuine notice arrives by post, at the registered keeper's address. Sanef, operator of the A13 and A14, states that it never asks for a toll by text.
  • The link does not lead to the operator's website. In France, the only official websites are sanef.com (A4, A13, A14, A29, A131, A154) and paiement.aliae.com (A79).
  • The tone is urgent: 'final reminder', 'surcharge today', a small amount to make you click.
  • It asks for your bank card on a page opened from the text.

What to do

  1. Do not tap the link and do not reply.
  2. Check for yourself: type the official website address and enter your number plate. If no crossing appears, there is nothing to pay.
  3. If the text came to a French number, forward it to 33700, France's free number for reporting scam texts, then delete it.
  4. If you have already given your card details, call your bank to block the card.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an unpaid free-flow toll cost in France?

The toll plus €10 if you pay the notice within fifteen days, plus €90 within two months. Without payment or dispute, an increased fixed fine of €375.

A text says I owe an unpaid toll: is it a scam?

Very probably. A genuine notice arrives by post. Check by entering your number plate on the official website, without using the link.

How do I dispute a French free-flow toll fine?

With the dispute card enclosed with the notice, returned within two months with your documents: vehicle lent or hired, theft or sale, or another reason such as a toll already paid.

My car was lent to someone: who pays?

The registered keeper remains liable, unless they name the person who was driving (French Highway Code, article L121-2).

What happens if I never pay?

After two months, the €375 fine is collected by the French Treasury, and for a French-registered vehicle the sale can then be blocked. Beyond five offences in twelve months, it becomes a criminal offence punishable by €7,500.
